Mathematical Intuition & Mechanics

The Latent Variable Dilemma

In supervised learning, each point has an explicit category tag \(z_i\). But in unsupervised mixture models, \(z_i \in \{1,\dots,K\}\) is a latent (unobserved) variable indicating which Gaussian produced observation \(x_i\).

$$\ln p(X|\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^N \ln \left( \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \mathcal{N}(x_i|\mu_k, \Sigma_k) \right)$$

Notice the summation inside the logarithm. Because \(\ln(a + b) \neq \ln a + \ln b\), derivatives with respect to parameters couple all components together into non-linear equations with no closed-form solution.

E-Step: Responsibilities & Lower Bound

Instead of making hard 0-or-1 cluster assignments (like K-Means), the E-step calculates soft responsibilities \(\gamma_{ik}\) via Bayes' Rule:

$$\gamma_{ik} = P(z_i = k | x_i, \theta^{(t)}) = \frac{\pi_k \mathcal{N}(x_i | \mu_k, \Sigma_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \pi_j \mathcal{N}(x_i | \mu_j, \Sigma_j)}$$

Geometrically, \(\gamma_{ik}\) represents component \(k\)'s probabilistic "ownership share" of point \(x_i\). In our visualizer, point color is blended proportionally to \(\vec{\gamma}_i\).

M-Step: Weighted Analytical Updates

With responsibilities \(\gamma_{ik}\) fixed, the complex coupled sum unlocks into an easy weighted Maximum Likelihood problem:

$$N_k = \sum_{i=1}^N \gamma_{ik}, \quad \pi_k = \frac{N_k}{N}$$
$$\mu_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{i=1}^N \gamma_{ik} x_i$$
$$\Sigma_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{i=1}^N \gamma_{ik} (x_i - \mu_k)(x_i - \mu_k)^T$$

Every Gaussian updates its center to the center of mass of the data weighted by its responsibility, and expands/rotates its covariance ellipse to match the dispersion.

Monotonic Convergence Guarantee

By Jensen's Inequality for concave functions (\(\ln\)), we construct the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) \(\mathcal{L}(q, \theta)\):

$$\ln p(X|\theta) = \mathcal{L}(q, \theta) + \text{KL}(q(Z) \,\|\, p(Z|X, \theta)) \ge \mathcal{L}(q, \theta)$$
  • E-step: Sets \(q(Z) = p(Z|X, \theta)\), making \(\text{KL}=0\) so the lower bound touches \(\ln p(X|\theta)\).
  • M-step: Maximizes \(\mathcal{L}(q, \theta)\) with respect to \(\theta\).

Hence, \(\ln p(X|\theta^{(t+1)}) \ge \ln p(X|\theta^{(t)})\) is mathematically guaranteed to never decrease.
